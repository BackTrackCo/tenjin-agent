'tenjin-cli': patch
---

Raises the routing-fee ceiling from $0.003 to $0.25, prefers each answer's quote, and shows local routing totals. When the router offers both an answer's own quote and its fixed $0.003 quote, Tenjin pays the answer's own quote, even when it is below $0.003, and pays the fixed quote only when it is the only offer. A quote above $0.25 is not paid, and the fixed quote is not used in its place. A dear answer that the routing funds cannot cover gets its deposit on the same call, so the offer is kept; a wallet that cannot fund that deposit pays nothing for the answer, and the next answer is still asked for. Deposits stay at most $0.25 and count toward your spend limits once, when sent; no spend limit changes.

`tenjin status` keeps its short Balance, Today, Accounts and Settings layout. Its JSON keeps remaining routing credit and lifetime cost rather than an estimate of calls left; `tenjin balance` shows cost beside the funds set aside. The cost is the deposits sent less the credit they still hold, a local estimate from the routing channel files with no extra network read. Payment-refusal notices describe the unpaid fee and keep free routing available, rather than saying routing stopped. A free answer does not clear a wallet warning; only a completed signed routing payment says “Paid routing is working again.”, without claiming funds were added. An install that did not approve limits on these terms is told them once, on its first routing call after the update, in either the hook or the request tool, with a shared once-only marker. Browse, inspection and selected-tool calls do not consume that notice. Setup keeps its spending question, choices, 15% recommendation fee and 25¢ ceiling, with shorter fee, deposit and upgrade wording. The linked reference explains the $0.001 minimum for routing a recommendation on a connected account, even when the tool bills its account plan; the one-time notice still names the ceiling. A missing wallet or signing block still allows the next request to reach the router for a free answer; another paid challenge keeps the block without trying the wallet again.
